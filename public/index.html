Epoxy flooring in Desert Ridge

The floor is the largest surface in any room. It should be designed like one.

Desert Ridge homes and businesses are built with intention: clean lines, large windows, carefully chosen materials. Yet the concrete under garages, patios, studios and service spaces is often treated as an afterthought — left bare, painted, or sealed with something that wears out in a season.

A professionally installed coating gives those surfaces the same consideration as the rest of the architecture. It can be quiet and tonal, crisp and graphic, or rich with metallic depth. Underneath the finish, though, the priorities are always practical: thorough diamond grinding, proper repairs, moisture testing and a system of layers chosen for the way the space is used.

We work across north Phoenix — from homes in Aviano, Fireside and Tatum Ranch to offices, showrooms and retail around the Loop 101 and SR-51. Every project starts with a visit, a conversation about the space and a written plan.

Whether the brief is a quiet satin floor for a gallery-like great room, a graphite flake garage that matches custom cabinetry, or a durable lobby finish for a busy medical building, the conversation starts the same way: how the space is used, what surrounds it and how it should feel when you walk in. The system follows from those answers.

Why choose us

A considered process from consultation to cure.

  1. Design-led recommendations

    We look at the architecture, the light and the materials around the floor, then suggest finishes that belong — whether that means a tonal satin, a refined flake or a metallic statement.

  2. Preparation that shows in the result

Residential epoxy flooring

Interiors with a continuous, quiet surface.

Coated concrete is increasingly chosen as a finished interior floor in modern homes. With no grout lines or seams, it lets a space read as one uninterrupted plane — especially striking in open plans where walls of glass meet the floor. It’s durable underfoot, simple to clean and comfortable in the heat of a Phoenix summer.

For living spaces we favor low-sheen finishes in warm grays, soft greige and pale stone tones, sometimes with a fine micro-flake for texture. Studios, gyms, wine rooms and media rooms are natural candidates, as are casitas and converted garages. Every interior project is prepared with extra care, because light raking across a satin floor reveals what a garage floor would hide.

Garage epoxy flooring

The garage as an extension of the house.

Many Desert Ridge garages are as carefully planned as the homes they belong to: cabinetry systems, slatwall, LED panels, EV chargers and, sometimes, a car worth showing off. Bare concrete undermines all of it. It stains, dusts and never looks finished, no matter how organized the walls are.

Our garage systems give the floor the same level of finish. A full-flake broadcast in a graphite or granite blend is the most practical everyday choice, hiding dust and tire marks with a subtle texture. Solid satin colors suit minimalist garages. For collector spaces, a high-gloss solid or metallic floor turns the garage into a showroom. Every exposed floor is sealed with a UV-stable polyaspartic topcoat so colors stay true when the door is open to the afternoon sun.

If you charge an EV at home, we coat carefully around the charging equipment and recommend a heavier build in the parking bay that sees daily use.

Installation process

Four phases. No shortcuts.

  1. Phase 01

    Consult

    On site, we review the space, the light and the use, test moisture, check for sealers and post-tension conditions, and present physical samples. You receive a written, itemized proposal.

  2. Phase 02

    Prepare

    Surfaces are protected, the slab is diamond-ground with dust extraction, and cracks, joints and imperfections are repaired and ground flush.

  3. Phase 03

    Build

    Primer, body coats and the decorative layer — flake, solid color or hand-worked metallic — are applied with controlled cure times between each.

  4. Phase 04

    Finish

    A UV-stable or chemical-resistant topcoat sets the sheen and texture. We walk the floor with you and provide care guidance.

Patios & outdoor concrete

Terraces and pool decks, refined.

Outdoor spaces in Desert Ridge are designed for long evenings outside — pool terraces, covered loggias, outdoor kitchens and courtyards. A coating gives tired or stained concrete a clean, consistent finish in tones that complement stucco, stone and landscape.

Outdoors we specify UV-stable materials, lighter reflective colors that stay more comfortable underfoot, and textures that grip when wet. Movement joints are left free to flex, so the slab can breathe without splitting the coating. Learn more about our patio and concrete coatings.

Local flooring considerations

Designing for north Phoenix conditions.

Sun and glass

Large windows, sliding walls and west-facing garages expose floors to intense UV. Standard epoxy ambers under that light; we specify aliphatic topcoats for any floor the sun can reach and help select colors that age gracefully.

Summer slab temperatures

Coatings cure faster on hot concrete, shortening working time. We schedule early starts, adjust product speeds and mix smaller batches so metallic and solid finishes stay even.

Post-tension foundations

Much of the area was built from the 1990s onward on post-tension slabs. Every bit of prep happens at the surface; nothing penetrates the foundation.

Dust and monsoon storms

Fine dust finds its way into garages and onto terraces. Mid-tone flake hides it, satin finishes show less of it, and textured outdoor surfaces stay secure after a summer downpour.

Community guidelines

Many neighborhoods have HOAs. Interior and garage floors rarely need approval, but visible exterior surfaces might. We provide samples and data sheets on request.

Occupied commercial spaces

Offices and retail rarely close. We plan after-hours phases and coordinate with property management so floors are finished without disrupting business.

For designers, architects & builders

A flooring partner that fits into the larger project.

A significant share of our work in Desert Ridge happens inside larger projects — a whole-home remodel, a new casita, a tenant build-out for a medical suite or showroom. In those settings the floor is one element in a sequence of trades, and timing matters as much as technique. Installed too early, a new finish gets scuffed by ladders and lifts. Installed too late, it delays move-in or opening day.

We work directly with interior designers, architects, general contractors and property managers to keep that sequence on track. Before the job starts, we review finish schedules, confirm colors and sheens on approved sample boards, and test the slab so moisture or leveling needs are known early. During construction, we coordinate dates with the other trades and provide protection guidance for anyone working after us.

Documentation is part of the service: product data and safety sheets for submittals, certificates of insurance for building management, and a written scope that matches the specification. The goal is simple — a floor that arrives on schedule, looks exactly as designed and doesn’t create surprises for anyone else on the project.

FAQ

Questions we’re asked most.

Can a coated floor look high-end enough for a modern home?

Yes — when the finish is chosen for the architecture. Satin solid colors in warm gray or greige read as refined and quiet; metallic finishes can resemble honed stone; fine flake can echo terrazzo. The key is careful preparation so the surface is flat and free of telegraphed cracks, because premium finishes show every flaw.

What do you recommend for a garage with an EV charger?

A full-flake or solid system with a polyaspartic topcoat works well. We seal neatly around the charger pedestal or wall unit, keep coatings away from electrical connections, and can add a slightly heavier build where the car parks and turns every day.

Is a glossy garage floor slippery?

High gloss can be slick when wet. We can add a fine clear grit to the topcoat that preserves most of the shine while improving grip, or choose a satin finish that reads more architectural and hides fine scuffs.

How long will my garage be out of service?

Rapid-cure polyaspartic lets most garages wrap up in a single visit; you can walk on it overnight and park a few days later. Metallic and multi-layer interior systems take longer, typically three to four days.

Do you work on post-tension slabs?

Yes. Many Phoenix homes built since the 1990s sit on post-tension foundations. Our preparation is surface grinding only — we never saw-cut, core or drill — so the tensioned cables are never at risk.

Can you install in occupied offices or retail spaces?

Yes. We schedule nights, weekends and phased work, coordinate with property managers, and use lower-odor products where they meet the performance requirements.

Does my HOA need to approve the work?

Interior and garage floors usually don’t. Visible exterior surfaces like front entries or courtyards may fall under your community’s guidelines. We can supply samples and product data for an approval request.

How should I maintain a showroom-style floor?

Dust mop with microfiber daily or as needed, damp mop with a neutral cleaner, and use walk-off mats at entries. Grit is what dulls a gloss finish, so keeping it off the floor is most of the work.
